# Asset provenance

Everything in this cell is either vendored from a public, licensed source or
built here from primitives.  Nothing was hand-copied from a screenshot.

1.99 MB in total, 16 files.  **Resolution cap**: the brief is "max 1080p, not more", so
no texture may exceed 1920x1080, and `make_textures._check_res()` is called on
every write so a generator default cannot quietly break the limit.  Only the
concrete floor - the one surface that fills the frame - spends the whole
budget; everything else is 1024 or smaller.

**Tile sizes.**  No material carries its own `texrepeat`.  After `paint()`,
`scene_look._fit_textures()` gives each textured geom a material clone whose
`texrepeat` lays a square tile - the size in `scene_look._TILES`, metres per
material - over that geom's biggest face, with `texuniform = false`.  Measured
on the compiled model: **408** textured per-face geoms, worst tile aspect
**1.000**, i.e. no geom stretches a texture whatever its proportions.  With
those tile sizes a 1024 px texture lands at about 0.5 mm/pixel on the line
frame (0.5 m tile) and 2.0 mm/pixel on the walls (2.0 m tile).

The FLOOR is the one geom this does not describe: it is the robot file's
infinite `plane`, so it has no face to fit a square to, and MuJoCo maps a plane
in WORLD units scaled by `texrepeat`.  `_geom_repeats` now returns `1/tile` for
a plane, so `_TILES["m_floor"] = 1.0` is a real 1 m slab panel rather than a
comment.  Measured on the overhead render: the concrete's joint grid lands
about 280 px apart on the floor, which is a ~1 m tile at that camera's scale.
(The old entry said 3.0 m; the plane branch used to return `(1, 1)`
unconditionally, so the number had never been in control.)

### Why the first texture pass looked wrong (measured, then fixed)

Three separate causes, all found by measuring rather than by eye:

* **`_to_img` normalised every field to full range.**  It rescaled each
generator's output to 0..255, so a field that is *meant* to vary only between
0.87 and 0.93 (brushed aluminium) arrived as a hard black-and-white pattern.
On a 190 mm motor barrel that read as camouflage paint, and on the vertical
posts as a candy-cane stripe.  The generator's intended level is now
preserved; measured spreads after the fix are 1-10% for surfaces and 75.6%
for the hazard stripe, which really is a stripe.
* **The gearmotor was pointed at the wrong texture.**  `m_motor` used the
brushed-aluminium texture, whose 260 streak cycles per tile alias into
salt-and-pepper mottle at that tile size.  Castings now use `t_cast`
(`m_cast`), a lower-frequency sand-cast look.
* **`wood()` tiled as blotches** because its grain phase varied by 9 radians
across the tile, so the seams never lined up.  Phase is now correlated.

### Value hierarchy: the TEXTURE is the colour, not the material (measured)

This one cost the most and is the most useful thing in this file.  Masking each
geom's own pixels out of a 1920x1080 render (`look_check.py` prints the table)
is how it was found, and the answer was not what the material fields suggest:

* **A material that carries an RGB texture ignores its `rgba` completely.**
  Measured twice, independently.  `m_floor` rendered **231.2/255** with its
  `rgba` at 0.95 and **231.2** with it at 0.47; the `m_alu` frame rendered
  152 counts at rgba 0.95, at 0.80 and with the whole family blackened to
  `0,0,0,1`.  Meanwhile scaling the *concrete texture* by 0.5 moved those same
  floor pixels to 117.6, and by 0.25 to 58.5 - linearly.  So the surface colour
  of a textured material is the texture's own level, and value tuning belongs
  in `make_textures.py`.
* **The reverse also holds**: materials with no RGB texture - floor markings,
  the parts, glazing, lamp lenses - do show their `rgba` (the aisle yellow and
  the coloured cubes in the stills are exactly their geom colours).
* So the lever that actually fixed this scene's flatness was the concrete
  texture's level: at the old "pale matrix" (mean 0.78) the floor rendered at
  231, the brightest surface in the frame and brighter than the machine (152)
  and the wall (142), which is why nothing separated.  At mean ~0.41 the floor
  renders ~122-126 and the ladder reads the way it should: **machine > wall >
  floor**.  Concrete at 0.78 albedo was never going to look like a factory
  floor.
* **The line's second level fix, same mechanism, different surface.**  The
  extruded frame's texture sat at mean 0.90 and measured **146** rendered -
  brighter than the wall (142) and the floor (123), so the machine read as
  white plastic.  Mill-finish 6063 extrusion is 0.60-0.70 diffuse; at 0.70 the
  frame measures **116**, i.e. the machine is now 25 counts DARKER than the
  wall it stands in front of, which is what makes the yellow robot and the
  belt read against it.  The chequer plate under the feet had the same
  problem in miniature: at 0.66 it measured **173**, the brightest surface in
  the whole frame - a row of white paddles - and at 0.36 it measures **100**.
  Three of the fifteen textures have now been retuned this way (concrete, alu,
  chequer); the rule is general, so a new texture's default level is a look
  decision, not a detail.
* The measured ladder after those fixes, from `look_check.py` on the compiled
  model (masked per-material means, drive view): wall 142 > floor 123 >
  **frame 116** > legs 108 > motor 102 > castings 85 > safety orange 76 >
  belt 36.  The building is brighter than the machine and the belt is the
  darkest thing in the frame, which is exactly the ordering a photograph of a
  real line has.
* `roughness` and `shininess` are **ignored** by the offscreen renderer (see
  the lighting notes), so the "specular is blowing it out" theory - which sent
  several edits in the wrong direction - was never testable, let alone true.
  A "light gain" of 270-530 was measured early on by dividing rendered pixels
  by material `rgba`, but that ratio is meaningless for a textured material:
  it was dividing by a number the renderer never used.
* The headlight finding stands: at XML-default levels it added an
  albedo-independent floor of about +70 counts, and the eight-light rig makes
  it redundant.

### Texture mapping (measured - this is what made the walls look stretched)

`texuniform` decides how a 2D texture lands on a geom, and the two modes are
not two flavours of the same thing:

| mode | mapping | consequence here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `false` | texture 0..1 lands on ONE GEOM FACE, `texrepeat` scales it | tile size = face size / texrepeat, identical in both directions. Used by every material. |
| `true` | object-space: s runs along the geom's local X, t along its local Y | on a WALL (a box 150 mm thick in y) the visible face varies in x and z, and z is never mapped - the texture tiles horizontally and is smeared to nothing vertically. Same for the roof slab. |

Measured with a bar-pattern texture on the real wall and roof boxes: per-face
mode gives exactly `texrepeat` repeats across and down a face (6.2 m wall at
`texrepeat (3, 1.5)` = 2.07 x 2.13 m tiles, near square); texuniform mode on
the same wall gave 9 repeats across and **zero** vertical repeats.  The roof
slab had the same defect, so the whole ceiling was one smeared smear of paint
texture.  Every material is now `texuniform = false`.

### No stretched textures: repeats are computed per geom

A repeat count that lives on the *material* is shared by every geom using it,
and those geoms are different shapes - so a single repeat still stretches the
texture on all but one of them.  Concretely, at `(3, 1)` the 6.2 x 1.05 m dado
band got 2.07 x 1.05 m tiles (a 2:1 stretched pattern, which is exactly what
the joint grid drawn into `wall_1024.png` then showed), and a 5.9 m beam got
2.95 x 0.35 m tiles while a 3.7 m column got 0.07 x 1.8 m ones.

So the material no longer carries the repeat at all.  `scene_look._TILES`
gives each material a TILE SIZE IN METRES, and `_fit_textures(spec)` walks the
painted geoms and clones the material per geom with

    tile  = longest side of the geom's dominant face
    n     = round(tile / _TILES[material])            (clamped to 1..16)
    texrepeat = (n * a / longest, n * b / longest)    a, b = the face's sides

which makes the tile square to rounding error on the dominant face of every
textured geom.  Checked on the compiled model over all 133 textured geoms:
largest tile aspect **1.000** (walls 2.067 x 2.067 m, dado band 1.185 x 1.185,
roof deck 1.550 x 1.550, belt 0.159 x 0.159, side panels 0.605 x 0.605).
A box's thin edge strips cannot all be square at once with one material, and on
this model every such strip is between 8 and 30 mm wide - the frame's panel
tops and the conduit sides - so nothing visible is left stretched.

### The first attempt at the frame, and why it was replaced

The first line was eight 50 mm box legs under each guide and two flat 8 mm side
plates, with box braces between the legs.  It met every geometric requirement -
it carried the belt, nothing floated, nothing interpenetrated - and it still
read as boxes, because it was boxes: a 50 x 50 solid post under a 10 mm plate,
with the legs 50 mm inboard of the beam they carried so the load path stepped
sideways, and a flat plate where an extrusion should be.  Measured, before the
redesign: the frame rendered at 146/255 - brighter than the hall wall (142) and
the floor (123), i.e. white plastic.

What replaced it is a real machine cross-section: two extruded side beams with
the belt between them and its underside on their tops, T-slot down the outer
face, T-slot screws, tube legs on levelling feet in the beams' own plane, tie
bars, a tail take-up, and fences rather than tunnels for the guards.  Measured
after: the frame renders at 116, the wall at 142, the floor at 123.

### The machine still read as a slab, and it was the texture (measured)

The redesign fixed the structure and the machine still rendered as grey sheet.
Two measurements found why, and neither of them was geometry:

  * `alu_1024.png` was a near-flat fill: mean 178.9, **sd 2.4/255** before the
    fix and 8.2 after it (p5-p95 went from 8 counts to 27).  A material with
    almost no variation renders as untextured plastic at every distance, so
    "grey slab" was an absent-texture problem, not a value problem.
  * the LEGS were drawing about a tenth of `steel_1024.png`, whose pattern is
    ~5 px vertical stripes: on a 50 mm tube face that magnifies into
    corrugation - a bundle of ribbed tubes where a square tube should be.  A
    coarse pattern on a small face is always a magnification and always wrong,
    so the legs, tie bars and cable duct now carry `alu_dk_512.png`: the same
    fine brush as the frame at a darker level (0.52), which is what a
    different extrusion actually looks like.

The level was deliberately NOT changed.  The frame still renders 116 against a
floor at 123 - i.e. it is darker than the floor it stands on - and it now reads
anyway, because a 27-count grain, a dark T-slot recess, dark legs (79), bolt
heads and brackets separate it by structure and texture rather than by one flat
value.  Chasing the value instead was already tried: 0.90 rendered at 146 and
read as white plastic.

### Lengthening the belt (200 mm, at the tail only)

The belt was extended from 1.43 m to 1.63 m between the roller crowns by moving
the TAIL roller from x = -0.74 to x = -0.94 (`assets/conveyor.xml`).  The head
end is untouched, so the discharge point, the bin, the drive unit and the whole
verified pick window keep their measured coordinates, and the infeed run is the
only thing that got longer - which is the only place a change is free, because
the arm cannot reach x < -0.2 in any pose.  `SPAWN_X` moved with it, to -0.72
(the flare mouth, under the new infeed fence).  `verify_cell.py` section 7
re-measured the transport over the longer run: 0.0989 m/s against a 0.10 m/s
belt, still discharged into the bin.

### Access window in front of the robot

The robot picks across the table edge, so the belt section it works in has to
be free of machinery.  Two windows, both deliberate:

| window | x range | why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| vendored guides (`rail_*`) | -0.02 .. +0.22 | fixed by the IK sweep: the wrist can only turn top-down into the lane with the guides interrupted here |
| frame (guards, fences, photo-eye) | -0.16 .. +0.36 (`build_cell.PICK_OPEN`) | the openings are cut wider than the guides so they read as access rather than as a gap; the fences sit outside it (x <= -0.62 and >= +0.54) and the photo-eye was moved past its far edge |

Inside `PICK_OPEN` nothing at all stands proud of the belt, and - new with this
redesign - nothing of the frame rises above 0.575 (the belt's underside) even
OUTSIDE the window either.  The frame's top IS the belt's underside, so the
region the arm was verified in cannot be occupied by anything added here.

`verify_cell.py` re-checks all of it every run: no static pair may
interpenetrate and nothing may float, so every one of these had to be given an
exact tangent contact with the part that carries it.  Two rounds of that check
caught real mistakes: leg braces that ran through the rails, and flare guides
that ran through the tail bearing blocks.

### The penetration test had to be rebuilt before it could be trusted

`mj_geomDistance` **cannot** be used here.  Measured on 3.14: two coaxial
cylinders that merely share a face report -5 mm, the same pair pulled 5 mm
apart reports 0, and cylinder-vs-box sharing a face reports -10 mm, while
box-vs-box is correct.  The old gate passed only because the scene used to
contain four cylinders that touched nothing; adding real hardware with hundreds
of them produced 82 impossible interpenetrations.  A replacement SAT kernel
was complete for boxes but **not** for curved pairs - it reported a 3.5 mm
penetration between a motor-foot bolt and the motor barrel that are genuinely
0.48 mm apart.

The gate now combines the two, because each is wrong alone: SAT supplies the
signed separation and is exact where its axis set is complete, and the verdict
comes from exact containment (surface plus interior samples, cross-checked both
ways, so one geom swallowed whole by another still counts).  Validated against
17 hand-computable cases - coincident boxes, a 0.6 mm sliver, separated pairs -
before it was let near the cell.  On the real cell that reduced 71 reported
pairs to **4 true overlaps**, all the same arithmetic slip (a flange, a bolt
pair and a pedestal that each grew outward from the wrong face) and all now
fixed at an exact tangent.  The gate passes with zero penetrations and zero
floating parts.

Detail pass roster, from the compiled model: 16 motor fins, 8 fan-cowl slots,
the gearbox breather and sight glass, a spider coupling with two hubs, an
encoder, 48 chequer treads, 68 fasteners (panel, pedestal, nameplate, terminal
lid and set screws), hazard and rating decals, cast motor feet on a pedestal,
and the field cable with its grommets.  The second pass added 10 leg brackets,
20 bracket bolts and 20 T-slot screws around the line's own frame (547 geoms in
total, 486 of them static).

## Hall

No open-licensed MJCF factory hall was found, so `factory_hall.py` builds one
from boxes: floor with aisle markings and safety hatching, walls with a dado
band and per-bay strip windows, steel columns, a roller shutter door, primary
beams, purlins, roof deck, lamp fixtures with real MuJoCo lights, a pallet and
crates.  It is original work and is documented as such in that file's
docstring.

Two things about the shell are worth recording, because both were visible as
"the roof looks horrible":

* **The eaves were open.**  The walls used to stop at `WALL_H = 3.20` while
the roof slab started at `3.55`, leaving a 350 mm see-through band around the
whole hall.  The walls now run to the deck's underside (`EAVE = 3.67`), the
beams were shortened to butt into the end walls instead of running inside
them, and the purlins and deck close the top.  Proven rather than eyeballed:
400 rays out of the hall in three directions now hit geometry 400/400 times
(they used to escape through that band).
* **The ceiling was dark by construction.**  The roof material was a mid-grey
(`rgba 0.52`) with the wall texture on it, and no light in the rig can reach
the ceiling - a spot's cone never gets above its own fixture.  The deck is now
a light liner (`rgba 0.90`) with a little self-emission, which measures at
~115/255 in the render where the old slab measured about half that.

### The roller door's texture (the "big door")

The 2.4 x 2.9 m roller shutter used to carry the galvanised-sheet texture - a
flat spangle field, tiled 1.2 m, which at door size is a big mottled panel with
no door in it.  It now has its own generator, `shutter_1024.png`: one vertical
profile - crown, face, rolling shadow, under-lip, lap line - repeated 14 times
per tile, plus a heavier section joint every 7 slats and a grime wash.  With
`_TILES["m_door"] = 1.0` the door's 2.9 m face takes 3 whole tiles, so the slat
pitch is a uniform **69 mm** and nothing is cut mid-slat at the top.

LEVEL is 0.60, and that number is measured rather than chosen: the door is the
one surface in the hall the light rig cannot reach (it is on the east wall at
x = 2.95 and every lamp, sky dome and the key light are aimed at the cell), so
at a real shutter's grey (mean 0.46) the whole curtain rendered 20-30/255 - a
black rectangle you had to hunt for slats in.  Measured on the `preview`
camera, which is the view the cell is looked at from: the curtain now renders
**79.2** with a standard deviation of **25.0** (min 20, max 144), i.e. the slats
are actually modulated.  The door's own hardware is new too - a bottom bar on
a rubber floor seal, and a pull bar - because a curtain that lands on a bare
sheet edge is half the reason the old door read as a painted panel.

The door is also why `emission` was re-tested: `m_roof` uses it, so it was
checked directly (set `m_wall` to 0.0 and 0.5 through `build_spec()`), and it is
**live** - the wall went 85.3 -> 205.8.  The first attempt to test it on the
door reported "no effect" for a different reason: the camera was pointed at the
wrong wall (MuJoCo's free-camera azimuth puts the eye on the opposite side from
the obvious one), so the pixels being sampled were not the door's at all.  A
segmentation mask is the only way to be sure which pixels you are measuring.

## The live pick cycle (`pick_cycle.py`)

The viewer runs the arm continuously: it takes every part the line indexes to
the grasp window and puts it in the blue tote.  Measured on this model, the
budget that shaped the design:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| `mj_step` (547 geoms) | **0.16 ms** |
| `ArmIK.solve`, defaults: 9 seeds x 30 passes x 40 iters | **2150 ms** |
| `ArmIK.solve`, warm start, `outer=1 iters=8 restarts=0` | **16 ms** |

The physics is free and the solver is not, so a control loop that solved IK at
every waypoint cannot hold real time - and the one-shot `demo_pick.py` spends
about ten minutes of solver time per cycle, which is fine for a script and
hopeless for a viewer.  So the cycle **plans once and replays**: `PickPlan`
solves a joint trajectory for the whole cycle (1.1 s, measured) and
`PickCycle` replays it by interpolating between waypoints, which costs a lerp
per sim step.  Real time is then a property of the construction, and the speed
is a dial rather than a baked-in number.

Three measured traps, all found by the plan's own audit:

  * **The arm has a hole in its workspace.**  A straight line from the tote
    back to the lane at z = 0.84 passes within 50-70 mm of the robot's own
    axis, where a 5-DoF arm cannot keep the site: measured **99 mm** of IK error
    on that line, against 0.4 mm when the same move is routed via the
    `HOME_XYZ` transfer pose (0.26, 0.18, 0.80).  Both the carry and the
    return go round the back now.
  * **A solver that is allowed to search will change branch.**  Left to pick
    the smallest error, it hops to the other elbow solution between two
    waypoints - measured **146 and 194 deg** of joint travel across a 20 mm
    step, which no interpolation can follow.  `restarts=0` leaves it one seed,
    where the arm already is, and the audit's joint jump went from 194 deg to
    **14 deg** (2.9 deg per sim step at this replay rate).
  * **A transfer may miss its line, a grasp may not.**  The air legs are
    allowed 20 mm of error (the part is held firmly and the next leg re-solves
    from where the arm actually is); the legs that end on a grasp or inside the
    tote are held to 0.5 mm.  Measured on the shipped plan: 0.10-0.50 mm on the
    pitch legs, 1.3-1.9 mm on the transfers.

Where the parts stop: the indexing stop parks a part's centre at
**0.0960-0.1005** (9 stops, 4.5 mm spread), so the descent aims mid-spread.

Measured over 60 s of sim: **12 placed, 9 removed, 0 missed**, cycle time
**4.3 s** (min 3.3, max 6.2) against ~8 s of belt travel from the infeed, with
2 parts in flight so the arm is almost never waiting.  The tote holds 3 and a
fourth arrival parks the oldest, so the part count in play is constant
(`conveyor_env.MAX_ACTIVE = 5` = 3 in the tote + 2 in flight) - that is what
keeps the scene from filling up.

## Parts

The parts are 32 mm boxes.  That size is a measured limit, not a preference:
`fixed_jaw_box3`, the plate that carries the fixed finger, is rigid with the
palm and sits 7.5 mm below the grasp site, so the hand can only pass a part

## Lighting notes (renderer facts of life, MuJoCo 3.14)

Measured on this setup, documented here because they bite:

* The renderer honours at most **8 lights**; lights after index 7 are
  silently ignored.  The cell uses exactly 8: 4 hall high-bays, the key spot
  (the ONLY shadow caster), the rim spot and two wide sky washes.
* `mjLIGHT_DIRECTIONAL` lights contribute nothing at all in this renderer.
  The robot's onboard light was therefore removed from the cell build.
* An **upward-pointing spot contributes nothing either**.  Built as two
  uplight fixtures aimed at the roof deck, and measured: the rendered picture
  is identical with them and with their `diffuse` forced to zero, at several
  positions, distances and cutoffs, while the same two lights pointing down
  change the same view by 80 luminance counts.  No light slot is spent on
  one; the deck is a light material instead.
* `light_intensity` is ignored by the renderer; brightness is `diffuse`.
* An MjSpec-created model compiles with an **all-zero headlight** (an XML
  model gets the usual ambient 0.1 / diffuse 0.4 defaults); `scene_look.polish`
  sets it explicitly - here to ambient 0.025 / diffuse 0.030 / specular 0.01,
  because at anything like the XML defaults the headlight adds an
  albedo-independent floor of about +70 luminance counts and the eight-light
  rig is already doing the work.
* `mjtMaterial.specular` and `shininess` have **no effect** in the offscreen
  `Renderer` on 3.14.  Measured: rebuilding the model with a material's
  `specular` forced to 0 (and `reflectance` to 0) renders a byte-identical
  image.  `rgba` and the rig do track.  Do not judge a look change by editing
  those two fields.
* Mutating `mat_rgba` on a **compiled** `MjModel` never reaches the renderer.
  Two A/B tests built on that assumption reported "no effect" and sent me
  looking for the wrong cause.  Any look experiment has to go back through the
  spec/source path.
* `MjvCamera.fovy` no longer exists in 3.14 - the frustum is derived from the
  viewport - so offline shot scripts must not set it.
* MjSpec-created lights default to `intensity = 0` (XML lights default to
  1) - another reason not to rely on intensity.
* The policy camera in `conveyor_env` renders with shadows off (4.6x faster,
  measured 48 ms vs 220 ms per 128 px frame); every presentation camera
  keeps shadows.  **Those two numbers are not comparable to the Phase 1
  benchmark below** - they were taken with the robot geom groups off, so the arm
  was never drawn.  Re-measured with everything visible: 1.0-1.1 ms/frame.

# Phase 0 findings: the wrist camera on MuJoCo 3.14.0

Appended by the sim-to-real vision BC pipeline, Phase 0 (camera remount and
verification).  Everything below is measured on this machine with
`time.perf_counter` / pixel counts, not inferred.  Scripts: `cam_variant.py`,
`check_camera.py`.

## The renderer, not the mount, was the hard part

Three separate defects in the offscreen `Renderer` had to be found before any
camera gate could produce a meaningful number.  All three are silent - they
return plausible images.

* **A camera declared on a moving body renders with its POSITION read from
  `cam_xpos` but its ORIENTATION left at the binding's default.**  The Python
  `MjvCamera` on 3.14.0 exposes only `azimuth`, `distance`, `elevation`,
  `fixedcamid`, `lookat`, `orthographic`, `trackbodyid`, `type` - no `pos`, no
  `quat`, no `fovy`.  `wrist_cam`, aimed exactly at the grasp site, covered
  **0.000** of the frame with the part at every `fovy` from 1 to 50 degrees,
  and the frame was filled by conveyor beams 0.7 m away and hall floor.  The
  position *is* honoured: adding 3 m to `cam_pos` changed the view.  Cameras on
  the **world** body render correctly (`overhead` does), so the fix is to
  compute the mount in the mount frame and write it into `cam_pos`/`cam_quat` in
  **world** coordinates each frame, with `cam_bodyid = 0`.  The camera is still
  carried rigidly by the arm.  A bonus is that `cam_fovy` works again, which it
  does not for free cameras.
* **`enable_segmentation_rendering()` ignores the requested camera.**  With the
  camera demonstrably pointed at the part, the RGB frame shows the part dead
  centre while the segmentation frame reports `crate_2` - a geom 2.7 m away -
  covering 52% of the frame.  Same result for a dedicated segmentation-only
  renderer and for either call order, and the raw values are `geom_id + 1`, so
  the offset is not the cause.  **Coverage is therefore measured by
  hide-and-diff**: render, re-render with the target geoms moved into a
  disabled geom group, count changed pixels.  Validated by rendering
  baseline / part-hidden side by side - the diff map silhouettes exactly the
  part and the part-hidden frame loses it.  **`look_check.py`'s per-material
  table is built on the broken call and its numbers should be re-taken.**
* **`Renderer._scene_option.geomgroup` defaults to `[1,1,1,0,0,0]`, which hides
  the entire robot.**  All 30 arm/gripper/jaw/camera-housing geoms are group 3
  or 4.  Any render of this cell that has not enabled them shows no arm at all,
  which is what made the first wrist-camera reads look like a view of empty
  floor.  Enable with `r._scene_option.geomgroup[:] = [1] * 6`.

Also worth keeping: `Renderer.scene.camera[0].pos` is **not** the render camera
(it carries a constant -34 mm offset in y here), so it cannot be used to verify
where a camera is.  Use `data.cam_xpos` for a world-parented camera, and verify
by looking at the image.

## The mount: geometry beats the spec

`wrist_cam` is declared on the menagerie `camera_mount` body (a child of
`gripper`), which also carries a physical housing: `wrist_camera_lens` at
mount-local `[-0.003, 0.0567, -0.0495]`.  Mount-local +Z points from the grasp
site back up the arm (the jaws travel along -Z), and +Y is **perpendicular to
the jaw-separation axis** - which is why the housing sits where it does, and why
tilting toward +Y looks in between the fingers instead of through one.

Measured part coverage, hide-and-diff, 32 mm part at the site, deg=45:

| standoff \ fovy | 50 | 45 | 40 | 35 | 30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 120 mm | .000 | .000 | .000 | .000 | .000 |
| 140 mm | .066 | .083 | .112 | .146 | .205 |
| 150 mm | .081 | .102 | .133 | **.177** | **.244** |
| 160 mm | .070 | .090 | .115 | .154 | .214 |
| 180 mm | .055 | .069 | .090 | .120 | .167 |
| 200 mm | .025 | .032 | .041 | .055 | .077 |

Two deviations from the Phase 0 spec, both forced by measurement:

* **Standoff 150 mm, not the planned 60-120 mm.**  At 120 mm and closer the lens
  is inside the gripper's own envelope and the part is **0.000 visible at every
  fovy**.  The nearest occluders sit 59-77 mm above the site with only 15-25 mm
  of lateral clearance; the gripper body itself occupies perp 15-52 mm all the
  way out to 120 mm.
* **fovy 35, not 50.**  The standoff is pinned by occlusion, which frees the
  field of view.  At fovy 50 the best achievable coverage is 8.1%, short of the
  15% gate; fovy 35 reaches 17.7% and fovy 30 reaches 24.4%.  8.1% vs the 15%
  target is a factor of 1.85, inside the 2x stop-and-report threshold, so the
  phase proceeded with the deviation stated rather than silently.

Tilt is nearly flat between 25 and 65 degrees (23.2-24.4% at fovy 30), so 40
degrees was taken as the middle of the planned 30-45 band.

## Cost warning for Phase 2

One replay of `demo_pick`'s expert takes **274-308 s wall** for ~8.4 s of
simulated time - about 36x slower than realtime - and only **0.7-0.9 s** of that
is rendering (2.6 ms per 256 px frame, much faster than the 50-66 ms measured
for the full-cell overhead view; a close wrist view frustum-culls most of the
cell).  The remaining ~99% is **IK**, not physics: `move_to` re-solves at 5 mm
Cartesian waypoints and `ArmIK.solve` runs `restarts=8` seeds x `outer=30` x
`iters=40` every time.

At 300 s/episode, 300 episodes on 4 workers is ~6.3 h, not the planned 15 min -
a 25x miss that has to be fixed before Phase 2 can hit its budget.  The obvious
lever is to keep the IK branch: once `move_to` is tracking a solution, one seed
(the current `q`) converges and the 8 random restarts are wasted. That is a
change to how `collect_state.py` drives the expert, not to the verified demo.

## A stale camera, found the hard way

`Mount.update()` originally wrote `cam_pos` / `cam_quat` into the model and
nothing else.  For a fixed camera the renderer does not read those - it reads
`data.cam_xpos` / `data.cam_xmat`, which only `mj_forward` refreshes.  So every
render used the camera pose from the *previous* physics step.

The symptom was nasty because it looked like physics: the same grasp
configuration, same joint angles, same gripper value, rendered a **blank dark
frame (mean luminance 16.5, part 0.0000)** on one call and **the part at
0.1747 (mean 63.3)** on the next, purely as a function of call order.  Two
cold IK solves that returned *identical* joint vectors disagreed with each
other, which is what finally ruled out an IK-branch explanation and pointed at
the render state.

The fix is exact rather than a second physics pass: the camera is
world-parented, so its world pose *is* `cam_pos`/`cam_quat`, and `update()` now
also writes `data.cam_xpos[cid]` and `data.cam_xmat[cid]` directly.  Three
identical repeats now return 0.1747 / 0.1623 / 0.1747 exactly.

Re-running the Phase 0 gate with the fix moved every number slightly the right
way, so the earlier figures were mildly pessimistic:

| gate | before fix | after fix |
|---|---|---|
| g1 part at grasp | 17.13% | **17.47%** |
| g2 in-cone frames seen | 100% (17/17) | 100% (17/17) |
| g2 strict, all frames | 71.43% | **75.00%** |
| g3 tote at place | 76.11% | 76.11% |

Rule of thumb this earned: when a fixed camera is repositioned by writing model
arrays, also refresh `data.cam_xpos` / `data.cam_xmat`, or the render is one
step behind and the error masquerades as scene content.

## Phase 2 pre-flight: the expert is fast enough, and the dataset would be empty

Two measurements taken before writing `collect_state.py`. Both contradicted the
plan's working assumptions.

### 1. The IK was never the blocker

`bench_ik.py`, 400 timed iterations each on the live model:

| primitive | us/call |
|---|---|
| `mj_kinematics` | 16.60 |
| `mj_kinematics` + `mj_comPos` | 11.00 |
| `mj_jacSite` (preallocated) | 2.00 |
| `_dls` (preallocated eye) | 16.40 |
| `ArmIK._wrist_task` (whole row) | 91.95 |

MuJoCo is ~20 us of that; the other ~70 us is numpy scaffolding (`np.cross`,
fresh `np.zeros`, fresh `np.eye`, `np.linalg.solve` on a 3x3). The earlier
77.4 us / 24.7 us figures for `mj_kinematics` / `mj_jacSite` did not reproduce.

End-to-end, `expert.py` seeds 0-4: wall 3.03 / 4.98 / 3.21 / 3.79 / 4.15 s
(mean 3.83, worst 4.98), IK 1.94-3.43 s, 139 solves, all `PLACED IN TOTE`.
Projected: **300 episodes on 4 workers = 4.8 min mean, 6.2 min worst, against
a 15 min target - 2.4x to 3.1x of margin.** The one full 9-seed search costs
1.86 s; the remaining 138 single-seed solves cost 0.05-0.25 s *in total*.

The "37-43 s/episode" figures in the Phase 1 notes are stale and were wrong to
treat as the Phase 2 blocker. Physics (101 us/step) and rendering (1.03 ms) were
never close to binding either.

### 2. `reset(seed)` randomizes nothing - the dataset would have no variance

`check_determinism.py` hashes qpos+qvel+ctrl over every physics state of two
full scripted episodes:

```
 seed        states    sha256[:16]    part  placed   wall s   sim s
    0         2018   ef2a06b5714eb104     0    True     2.83   14.92
    7         2018   ef2a06b5714eb104     0    True     2.72   14.92
  identical across seeds : True
```

Byte-identical. `ConveyorPickEnv.reset()` forwards `seed` to `gym.Env.reset`,
which only advances `self.np_random`, and then rebuilds the world
deterministically: `mj_resetData`, arm to `HOME`, every part to `C.PARK`,
`_spawn()` writing a fixed `C.SPAWN_X, C.BELT_Y, BELT_TOP+CUBE/2` with an
identity quaternion, and `set_belt_speed(model, C.BELT_SPEED)`. Nothing in the
env ever reads `np_random`.

Consequences:

- `ds_0001` as planned would be 300 byte-identical copies of one demonstration.
- Full randomization cannot be inherited from the env; it has to be built in
  `collect_state.py`, which must therefore own a randomization vector and write
  it into `manifest.jsonl` per episode.
- The part rests at `[0.1441, -0.1828, 0.651]` every single time, and the
  indexing stop parks it at x=0.101 every time (5/5 seeds). The open jaws give
  17 mm a side, so a +-2 mm stop is free margin being wasted.

The real Phase 2 risk was never wall time. It is coverage.

## Phase 2: ds_0001 collected and verified

### The failure taxonomy was measured before it was tuned

Three diagnostics (`diag_grasp.py`, `trace_yaw.py`, `diag_yaw.py`,
`diag_align.py`) established, with full episodes:

- The first sampler's grasp failures were **close-time ejection**: with jaws
  oblique to a part face, the close shoves the part sideways (measured 13 mm
  in one burst, at the moment the jaw gap crossed the part's corner diagonal)
  and the expert then misses.
- With centred oblique grasps, failures move to **mid-carry slip**: corner
  contacts slide out between lift and tote (grasped 16/16, placed 0/8 at
  45/67.5/135/157.5 deg in the first sweep).
- Controlled yaw sweep at nominal settings: success is periodic in 90 deg and
  tracks jaw-to-face alignment, not yaw itself.
- Alignment grid (full-episode placed rate): EXACT face alignment 16/16 across
  all 8 yaws; +-0.03 rad jaw jitter 75-83%; aim jitter sigma 2 mm -> 92%,
  5 mm -> 67% (all carry slip, grasp always held).  Conclusion: the sampler
  keeps jaw alignment EXACT and spends the noise budget on part yaw (full
  circle), belt speed (0.6-1.4x), hover height (+-20 mm), aim sigma 1 mm,
  grip_shut +-1 mm.

### `conveyor_env` facts recorded en route (no file modified)

- `_cube_pos(i)` is raw free-joint qpos - ground truth, not a filtered estimate.
- `episode()` (both demo_pick and expert) sets `env._arm_lo/_arm_hi`; anything
  driving the env without `episode()` must set them itself.
- `RandEnv.__setattr__` routes writes to the real env: a plain wrapper's
  `env._sim_t += dt` SHADOWS the attribute and silently freezes the env's
  spawn clock at 0.

### Dataset

`ds_0001/`: 316 attempts -> 303 ok (95.9%), 13 dropped, all "grasp failed",

## Phase 3: deferred rendering - sample batch rendered and verified

### 🔴 Machine-state incident: renders silently dropped to the Intel iGPU

Mid-Phase 3 the render benchmark fell from 1.03 ms/frame to **66.67 ms/frame**
in an unmodified codebase, and the fresh context reported
`GL_RENDERER Intel(R) UHD Graphics`.  Cause: Windows' auto GPU heuristic had
started steering `python.exe` to the iGPU (no per-app preference existed).
Fix, no admin needed, per-app and reversible:

```
reg add "HKCU\Software\Microsoft\DirectX\UserGpuPreferences" \
  /v "C:\Users\gamee\AppData\Local\Programs\Python\Python312\python.exe" \
  /t REG_SZ /d "GpuPreference=2;" /f
```

After: `GL_RENDERER NVIDIA GeForce RTX 2060`, wrist camera **1.60 ms/frame**
(250 timed frames).  Rule earned: `bench_render.py`'s GL_VENDOR/RENDERER line
must be read on every render-heavy run; a 60x regression with zero code change
is machine state, and per rule 5 that is a STOP-and-report condition.

| training | batch 256 fp16 AMP 42 ms/step, peak 336 MiB alloc / 556 MiB reserved (torch); ~1.8 GB nvidia-smi whole process incl. context+desktop |

### Eval input bugs found and fixed (both were 0% pick causes)

1. `current_frame` returned **channel 0 of a 0..255 float tensor**: the trained
   cache is `mean(RGB)` in 0..1 (`torch area` resize, uint8, `/255` in
   dataset.py).  Measured against cache g[0]: mean|diff| 0.4 gray levels for
   `torch_mean`, 6.4 for red.  The 255x input scale saturated the policy
   (preds pinned at +-1.0).
2. Everything else about the input patch is now verified: rgb(96) vs gray(96)
   conversion, qpos order, normalisation, and the action decode round-trip
   (`decode(normalize(x)) == x` to 5.6e-17).

### Eval handover is bit-exact - the 0% is not a wiring bug

`probe_match.py` / `probe_cachestart.py` / `probe_diverge.py`:
- Re-running the collector sequence with the same seed+vector reproduces the
  recorded pose exactly: probe qpos == ds_0001 qpos at the held hover rows
  (dev 0), and after replaying the expert's own 31 act_raw rows the state
  equals `cache q[0]` to 4.4e-08.
- Control: replaying the expert's `act_raw` from that same state picks and
  places in 4.26 s -> harness, physics, renderer and start state are sane.
- In-distribution chunk accuracy is good: mean|err| 0.01-0.03 on cache rows.

### The cache window starts 0.155 s after the eval handover

Cache row 0 == ep row 1419 (maxdev 0.0) while the handover is ep row 1388.
The cache builder's "part-indexed row" is ~37 rows (0.185 s) later than the
collector's wait-loop break, so the first cache state is already mid-approach.
Starting the policy exactly at cache row 0 (bit-exact) still fails, so this
offset alone is not the blocker.

### Root cause (measured): the phase clock is millimetre-scale settling

`probe_rows.py`: the gt grip is a step (open -0.15 -> shut -0.80, one entry)
and the model predicts a **ramp over +-2 entries** at the transition.  The
phase is recoverable in-principle from the arm's settling residual (qpos at
parked rows 5..8 differs by 0.002 rad between rows while the gt close moves
from entry 7 to entry 3), but the rollout's own tracking error (2-6 mm,
0.04 rad) destroys that cue.  Result (`probe_diverge.py`): the policy closes
the grip early (pred -0.55 vs gt -0.15 at t+0.6 s) and lifts early (site 2-6
cm above the expert's parked pose exactly when the close is due), so the jaws
shove the part 9-20 mm instead of pinching it (the same close-time ejection
the Phase-2 taxonomy measured), after which the state (half-closed jaws, no
part) is off-distribution and the arm parks at the tote for the rest of the
30 s horizon.  Arm tracking before that moment is good (|site dev| <= 9 mm).

### Training frames carry a random 0-100 ms latency, eval does not

The Phase-3 render stores `img_row` with `u ~ U(0, 100 ms)` staleness.  Eval
fed fresh frames, i.e. the model saw a state *later* than the one it learned
for that qpos and fired the close early.  Mimicking the staleness in eval
(stack shifted back 0..2 frame slots, `probe_lat.py`) removed the t+0.6 s
false close (pred -0.12 instead of -0.55) but not the early lift, so this is
one real mismatch (now fixed in evaluate.py) and not the whole story.

### Phase 5 result

50-seed harness fixed; screening runs (n=5, both conditions) still 0% pick /
0% place with the fixed inputs, and the expert-replay control picks 100% from
the same start.  Verdict: the policy reproduces the expert's actions from
given states (val 0.0007, mean|err| 0.01-0.03) but cannot close the loop,
because transition *timing* is not observable from a 200 ms frame stack plus
millimetre settling residuals that its own errors erase.  Candidate Phase-6
fixes, cheapest first: (a) give the model an explicit phase/timer observable
(e.g. append time-since-motion, or make the trunk recurrent); (b) lengthen
the history (8 frames = 700 ms) so "the arm stopped 0.4 s ago" is directly
visible; (c) shorten the expert's settle before the close so the transition
is triggered by the observable motion stop rather than an elapsed delay
(needs re-collection + re-render + retrain).

## Phase 6: history length, the eval priming bug, and the real blocker

Three things were done: the phase structure was measured, the history length
was made configurable and an 8-frame model trained, and the grasp tolerance
was measured.  The third one changed the conclusion.

### Measured: the pre-close park (the Phase-5 "unobservable phase" claim)

`probe_phase.py`, all 303 rendered episodes (the cache rows are the whole
manipulation window: 51 rows = 5.1 s at 10 Hz):

- The gt close is at row 9-12 (median 10), i.e. ~1.0-1.2 s into the window.
- Before it the arm is stationary for **median 300 ms** (p10 0, p90 300, max
  400): 35 eps 0 ms, 54 eps 200 ms, 188 eps 300 ms, 26 eps 400 ms.
- Arm motion INSIDE the park: **0.00019 rad/row** mean, against the rollout's
  own tracking error of 0.04 rad - i.e. the error is **208x** the whole
  in-park motion.
- Image change inside the park 1.40 gray levels/row vs 23.38 before: 17x less.
- Hence: a 3-frame stack (200 ms) spans the park in **11.6%** of episodes,
  5 frames (400 ms) 29.4%, 8 frames (700 ms) **100%**.

### The eval primed the history with repeated frames (bug, fixed)

`evaluate.py` built the first input as `[f0] * N`, so the first decision saw N
IDENTICAL frames - a pattern no training sample ever produces.  `probe_prime.py`
(two checkpoints, one cache episode, no physics):

| input | s3 (3 frames) | s8 (8 frames) |
|---|---|---|
| PRIMED `[f0]*N` | ramps to -0.402, close at entry 3 | **-0.772, closes at entry 0** |
| REAL consecutive rows | -0.153, stays open | -0.157, stays open |
| teacher-forced close row | 10 (gt 9) | 9 (gt 9) |
| open-phase grip sd | 0.025 | 0.028 |

Both models read "the arm has been static for the whole window" as "parked,
close now".  Fixed: the buffer is filled with real frames captured at 50 ms
during the belt wait and the 0.4 s settle (`snap()`), never a repeated frame.
(A second, self-inflicted harness bug was introduced and fixed in the same
edit: `idx = [-1 - 2*k - d ...]` shadowed the part index `idx`, so
`_grasped() == idx` compared an int to a list and `picked` could never be True.
Every eval run between those two edits is invalid for the pick metric.)

### The 8-frame model (ckpt/policy_s8.pt) is worse in closed loop

History length is now configurable (`dataset.set_stack`, `Policy(img_ch=)`,
`train.py --stack`, stack saved in the checkpoint).  `STACK` also lives on the
dataset instance on purpose: Windows DataLoader workers are spawned, so a
runtime module global does NOT reach them and they silently fed 3-frame batches
to an 8-channel model.

- s8: 989,808 params, 30 epochs, best val **0.00041** (s3: 986,208, 0.00070).
  Better in-distribution, and its teacher-forced close row is exactly right.
- In closed loop it is unusable: from the collector handover it commands the
grip closed at **t = 0.00 s** and holds `gq` ~0.0-0.15 for the whole 26 s
  horizon.  The eval hands over 0.155 s BEFORE the cache window, where the arm
  is static at hover; over 700 ms that is exactly the training pattern for the
  pre-close park, the only static-arm state in the data.  s3's 200 ms window is
  short enough to stay ambiguous, so s3 keeps the close time roughly right.

### The real blocker: tolerance ~2-4 mm, the policy is 8-13 mm off

`probe_diverge6.py` from a bit-exact cache-row-0 start (dev 4.41e-08), s3:
grasp-site deviation from the expert at the grasp moment [-3.3, -9.2, -13.1] mm
and [+7.9, -0.2, -8.0] mm; the grip reaches only -0.44 (expert -0.797; gq 0.35
vs 0.21); the part moves **<= 2 mm** - the jaws shut in the air.

`probe_tolerance.py` runs the expert itself with the aim point moved outside
the collector's +-2 mm envelope (same seed and vector; only aim_xy changes):

| aim_xy +x (mm) | 0 | 1 | 2 | 2.5 | 3 | 3.5 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| placed | Y | Y | Y | Y | Y | Y | **N** | N | N | N | N |

So the expert's grasp survives < 4 mm of hand-placement error while the BC
policy sits 8-13 mm out - **2-3x outside the tolerance** - with the close
fired at roughly the right time.  Vertical offset is forgiving (up to 40 mm
still places); only xy is tight.  Caveat: re-running several episodes in one
process makes later episodes fail at 0 mm as well, so only the first episode of
a fresh process is trustworthy; the ep-0 curve above is that case and is
monotone (0,1,2,2.5,3,3.5 pass; 4 and above fail).

### Consequences

- The Phase-5 timing diagnosis is real and now quantified, and the priming bug
  was a second, independent defect - but neither is the binding constraint.
  Even a perfect phase oracle cannot grasp from 8-13 mm away.
- 0% pick is therefore the expected result, and pure BC is the wrong tool at
  this tolerance.  Options: widen the tolerance (funnel / chamfered part /
  compliant jaws, target >= 15-20 mm); close only the final grasp with a
  scripted or vision/force-servoed controller (BC for gross motion, classic
  control for the last millimetres); or buy precision with higher-resolution
  RGB, 1-entry open-loop execution and far more data.
- Also open: the eval/collector handover is 0.155 s before the cache window
  start - a state absent from training.  Start the policy at cache row 0, or
  extend the cache window back over the handover.

## Phase 7: the 0% pick root-caused to a 13 mm site offset, 25 Hz pipeline,
variance, barriers off

Four asks this phase: fix the 0% pick, put a little variance into where the
part stops, take the assembly line's barriers off so the arm works freely, and
raise the observation rate (24 Hz, or solve the fast-motion problem another
way).

### The blocker was NOT tolerance - it was a 13 mm offset between the aim point
### and the jaws

`probe_tol0.py` re-measured the expert's aim envelope with ONE episode per
fresh process (the old probe_tolerance.py ran every offset in one process and
its own caveat said only the first episode is trustworthy): 0 / +2 mm place,
+4 mm and beyond do not, and at +4 mm the close left the part on the belt
pushed 11.8 mm sideways with a jaw gap of 29.3 mm - i.e. the jaws closed PAST
the 32 mm part, it was not inside them.

`inspect_grasp_pose.py` (world-space dump at the first close command) shows
why.  The `grasp` site sits on the part (dx 0.7 mm, dy 1.9 mm) but the jaw
TIPS are both ~12-13 mm off the part's centre along the RING axis
(perpendicular to the closing axis): `build_cell`'s `gs.pos` is not the tip
midpoint at GRIP_OPEN.  The finger contact geometry is a stack of 2 mm thick
nubs 8-22 mm wide, so with the jaws that far off centre the moving blade only
clips the part's +ring corner: any additional error and it misses the face
entirely and the close launches the part out of the jaws.

* Fix: `collect_state.jaw_center_xy()`, a constant vector in the gripper frame
  (13.6 mm along the ring axis + 1.85 mm along the closing axis, both measured)
  added to every aim target.  Being gripper-frame, it rotates with the
  commanded jaw yaw and is valid at every part yaw.
* Measured after: the expert places at EVERY aim offset 0..20 mm (7/7) and the
  residual shove falls 9.7 -> 3.4 mm as the error grows - the signature of a
  symmetric, self-centring push.  Closing-axis offset: 4/4 at -16 mm.  The
  close now stalls at 32.5-32.8 mm of the 32 mm part = a real pinch.
* Grip channel: the gripper is bistable in every demonstration (0.64 / 0.02 =
  +0.1515 / -0.7974 normalised) and a regression head averages the transition
  (probe_rows.py: a ramp over +-2 entries; probe_diverge6: the close reached
  only -0.44 = 43 mm, so the jaws shut in the air even when they are in place).
  evaluate.py now threshold-decodes the channel (`GRIP_SNAP`, `--no-snap`
  ablation) so the close is commanded fully.
* Everything that moves the arm goes through `collect_state._patched_move_to`,
  so collector, renderer, evaluator and probes all inherit the correction.

### The other three asks

* Stop variance: `sample_vector` samples `stop_dx ~ U(-8, +8) mm` and
  `collect_state.apply_stop()` moves the drive's hold threshold per episode
  (`conveyor_env.X_STOP` is read as a module global at every step, so this is a
  runtime patch, not an edit of the verified env).  The renderer recomputes its
  window from the recorded vector and the evaluator installs the same value, so
  collection, rendering and eval agree.  ds_0001's single resting x (0.100 m
  every episode) is gone.
* Barriers: `build_cell.BARRIERS = False` deletes the two vendored side-guide
  pairs, both fence panels with their lips and decals, and the rail-mounted
  photo-eye - 16 geoms - from the compiled spec by name.  line_frame.py, the
  vendored conveyor.xml and every layout constant stay untouched and
  `BARRIERS = True` rebuilds the ds_0001 cell.
* 25 Hz: 24 Hz is not an integer number of 5 ms physics steps; 40 ms is exactly
  8, so the camera, the cache rows, the chunk entries and the eval decisions all
  run on 25 Hz.  `render_dataset.FPS = 25` with `LAT_MAX = 1/FPS` (worst-case
  observation staleness 2.4x smaller than the 10 Hz dataset), `dataset.GRID_MS
  = 40`, one chunk entry = 8 physics rows, eval `CAM_DT = 0.04` and `EXEC = 2`
  (80 ms open loop against the 10 Hz run's 200 ms).  The observation grid is
  now the policy grid, so the stack spacing and the eval's history cadence are
  the same number everywhere instead of the old 100 ms frames vs 50 ms
  decisions split.

### The cache builder was lost - rebuilt, validated against the surviving cache

`build_cache.py` was written from the alignment `probe_cache_align.py` could
measure and is validated by `--verify`, which rebuilds the 10 Hz rows from
`ds_0001` and diffs them against the surviving cache: obs times identical,
gray frames mean|d| 0.39 levels (uint8 rounding; 60% of pixels exactly equal),
chunks identical except at the grip transition (a full 0.646 step - one entry
of anchor difference) and q identical except at rows where the latency is
observable.  That difference is a real finding: the old cache anchored q and
the chunks at the STALE row (the state the image shows, t_j - u_j) while
`evaluate.py` has always fed a FRESH qpos and a stale image.  The new builder
anchors both at the nominal observation row; the eval reproduces the image
staleness with a 0..1 slot stack shift, which at 25 Hz is exactly 0..40 ms
(the old eval shifted 0..2 slots of 100 ms = up to 200 ms for a 100 ms
latency - a 2x mimicry error).  The old cache is kept as `cache_10hz/`.

### Eval-harness bugs found in this phase (all three changed the outcome)

1. **The frame-stack stride.**  After the cadence moved to 40 ms the stack
   selector still stepped 2 slots, so the model received an 80 ms-spaced, 560 ms
   history.  It read that stretched stack as "static for the whole window" -
   the Phase-6 priming signature - commanded the grip closed at the FIRST
   decision (from the hover pose) and drove to the tote with the jaws shut.
   Fixed: one slot per grid step.
2. **Where the window starts.**  With the window starting 0.4 s after the part
   arrived, the eval's first stack was filled with settle frames, which are
   bit-identical to each other because nothing moves during the settle.  The
   model's only "static for 280 ms" training pattern was the pre-close park, so
   it closed at t = 0 (probe_eval0_25.py: 8 identical frames, grip -0.49 at
   entry 0 against -0.17 when the same frames come from the cache).  The window
   now starts at the part's arrival: the settle is in the data with "hold open"
   as its ground truth, and the only difference from the park is what the image
   shows - the part 0.16 m away versus between the jaws.
3. **Ablations were incomparable**: the per-decision latency draw came from the
   same RNG as the episode draws, so changing EXEC/STACK changed WHICH episodes
   a run saw.  The latency now has its own stream and episodes are fixed across
   configurations.

### Result: 0% -> 33-42% pick, and the failure mode is now vertical timing

The 0% is gone in the sense that matters: the expert grasps from an aim error
three times larger than the policy's, so the policy is no longer geometrically
excluded, and the closed loop picks parts again.

| run (25 Hz, ds_0002, 306 eps) | nominal pick | nominal place | tierB pick | tierB place |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8-frame stack, EXEC 2 | 0/12 | 0/12 | 1/12 | 0/12 |
| 8-frame stack, EXEC 1 | 0/12 | 0/12 | 0/12 | 0/12 |
| **12-frame stack, EXEC 2** | **4/12 (33%)** | **3/12** | **5/12 (42%)** | **4/12** |
| 12-frame stack, EXEC 1 | 3/12 | 3/12 | 0/12 | 0/12 |

EXEC 2 (80 ms open loop) beats EXEC 1 in both stack lengths - a shorter loop
does not help because the error is the model's, not the execution's.  The
440 ms stack is what unlocked it: the expert's pre-close park is 300-400 ms
(Phase 6), so an 8-frame 280 ms history cannot show that the arm has stopped,
and a 12-frame 440 ms one can.  Two eval-side changes are part of the working
configuration: the bistable grip decode (`GRIP_SNAP`) and the 0.4 s grasp dwell
(after the close is commanded the ARM holds its last command, exactly as the
demonstrations do - `sim(0.5, SHUT)` then `sim(0.3, SHUT)` with the arm ctrl
held - so the lift cannot start in the same 80 ms window as the close).

Failure taxonomy of the remaining 8/12 (traced, `trace_s12.json`): every
success closes with the site within |dz| <= 7.4 mm of the part and grasps
within 160 ms (jaw gap 31-39 mm = a real pinch).  Every failure closes with the
site 16-25 mm ABOVE the part (the expert's measured vertical band is +-5-10 mm,
so those jaws shut in the air), and 3 of the 8 never descend at all - the close
fires at 0.56-0.8 s with the site 120-250 mm up, i.e. the model reads its own
static handover stack as "grasp already done" and heads for the tote.  So the
remaining work is timing, not geometry: a deeper grasp target (the expert's
measured band moves with it), a 600 ms stack, and more episodes are the three
levers the traces point at.

### Act 2: the failure taxonomy points one step deeper - ds_0003

The 33% run closes 16-25 mm above the part on every failure.  The expert's own
successful closes sit at a site height that is a fixed 0 mm offset from the
`grasp` site, and the measured vertical band is generous (`probe_tolerance.py`:
+40 mm of hover offset still places), so the demonstration can simply put the
close 10 mm LOWER without leaving the band.  Rationale: (a) a deeper close is a
stronger, earlier visual cue for the model - at the current height the jaw tips
and the part nearly coincide in the image, so "close now" and "keep descending"
are a few pixels apart; (b) it buys margin against the policy's residual height
error, since the failure direction is consistently high, not low.

`collect_state.py` now carries `GRASP_DZ = +0.010 m` in the aim vector
(`vector['grasp_dz']`, so it is per-episode data, not a hidden constant).
Per-episode variance on the stop position (`stop_dx ~ U(+-8 mm)`) is unchanged
from Act 1.

### A silent data/split mismatch cost one training run (found by asking what the numbers meant)

The first ds_0003 training run printed `episodes: train 258 val 30 test 15`,
which is ds_0001's split, not ds_0003's (309 episodes -> 264/30/15).  Two
defaults had been lying dormant since Phase 4:

1. `make_loaders(ds="ds_0001", ...)` and `train.py` never passed `ds`, so the
trainer read the **ds_0001 manifest** for its split no matter which data it was
meant to train on.
2. `CacheEp` had `np.load(f"cache/ep_XXXXXX.npz")` hardcoded, so the trainer
   read whatever was in `cache/` - at that moment the ds_0002 25 Hz cache.

Together they are invisible: episode ids overlap across ds_0001/0002/0003, the
row shape is identical, the loader is happy, and the run is simply the WRONG
DATA (the first launch at 21:56 reproduced the ds_0002 experiment under a
ds_0003 filename; killed at 22:02, log kept as `train_0003_s12_WRONGDATA.log`).

Fixed, with the check that would have caught it:

- `make_loaders(ds, cache_dir, ...)` - both required, no defaults to drift.
- `build_cache.py` writes a `ds` tag into every episode npz; `BCDataset`
  asserts the tag matches the manifest it was given.  Legacy untagged caches are
  rejected unless `allow_untagged=True` is passed explicitly (nothing passes it).
- `CacheEp` asserts the row spacing equals the grid, so a 100 ms 10 Hz cache
  on the 40 ms grid is now a loud failure ("rows are 100.0 ms apart, the grid
  is 40 ms") instead of a plausible-looking run.
- `cache/` (ds_0002) and `cache_0003/` (ds_0003) were re-saved with their tags;
  both directions of the guard are covered by a test in the session log
  (ds_0002 tag + cache_0003 dir rejected, matching pair accepted).

What this does NOT invalidate: the earlier 25 Hz ds_0002 run (the 0% ->
33-42% result) read the right cache - `cache/` held exactly that data - and
the split rule is by episode id, identical in every manifest, so its val and
test episodes were genuine held-out ds_0002 episodes; only ds_0002's last 3
train episodes (ids 303-305) were excluded because ds_0001's manifest ends at
302.  The eval numbers that carry the result are rollouts on the env, not val
loss, so the headline stands as measured.

`cache_0003` was also verified against its renders before being trusted: for 12
sampled episodes including the tail (300, 305..308), `g` is byte-exact versus
`ds_0003/imgs/*.npz` recomputed through `gray96` (12/12, 3.9 s), and rows ==
rendered frames == 143-144 per episode.

Corrected run: `--ds ds_0003 --cache cache_0003` (264 / 30 / 15, 34,797 train
samples, 992,688 params, ~25-29 s/epoch, best epoch 27 val 0.000129 ->
`ckpt/policy_0003_s12.pt`).

### Head-to-head at n = 50: the deeper close is falsified

Evaluated against `ckpt/policy_25hz_s12.pt` (ds_0002, 306 eps, epoch 29
val 0.00013) in the working configuration - 12-frame stack, EXEC 2 (80 ms open
loop), GRIP_SNAP, 0.4 s grasp dwell, barriers off - with both models on the
SAME episodes: the episode RNG is seeded 4242/4243 and nothing in the rollout
consumes it, so the counterfactual is exact.  The n = 30 runs are byte-for-byte
prefixes of the n = 50 runs (checked for both models and both conditions), so
the two sample sizes are one measurement.

| paired, same 50 episodes | 0003 pick | 0003 place | 0002 pick | 0002 place |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nominal | 15/50 (30%) | 9/50 (18%) | 15/50 (30%) | 13/50 (26%) |
| tier-B  | 7/50 (14%)  | 2/50 (4%)  | 20/50 (40%) | 12/50 (24%) |

Exact McNemar on the discordant pairs: nominal pick 7 vs 7, p = 1.00; nominal
place 5 vs 9, p = 0.42; tier-B pick 4 vs 17, p = 0.0072; tier-B place 1 vs 11,
p = 0.0063.

So the +10 mm deeper grasp buys NOTHING nominally - the n = 12 and n = 30
"33% -> 40%" readings were small-sample noise, and at n = 50 the two models
are indistinguishable on pick (7 episodes each way) - and it costs a
significant tier-B regression.  The Act-2 rationale ("a deeper close is a
stronger, earlier visual cue for the model") is falsified as stated.

Where the regression comes from: `probe_tierb_deep.py` classifies every
traced episode by what the JAWS did after the close command.  The 32 mm cube
makes the jaw gap a clean discriminator - a pinch stalls at 32-34 mm, an air
close runs down to ~18 mm - and no picked episode is ever in the air-close
class (the open grip is -0.151, the close command is the first decision whose
grip crosses -0.4).

| (n = 50 each) | close dz p50 | pinches | air closes | pinch->pick | pinch->place |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Two mechanisms, both in the same table:

1. **The two models use the grip channel completely differently.** ds_0002
   commands the close early, high above the part (median 78 mm up on the
   episodes that pinch) and lets the descent plus the 0.4 s closing stroke
   find the part.  ds_0003, trained on demos whose close is 10 mm deeper,
   commands it AT the part (median -3.7 mm on the pinches - jaw-tip level).
   A trigger that tight needs a look that exact: under tier-B visuals ds_0003's
   close moves 25 mm up (pinch-class p50 -3.7 -> +21.7 mm) and 37/50 episodes
   shut on air.  ds_0002's coarse trigger does not read the part's appearance,
   which is why its pinch count RISES under tier-B (20 -> 29) and its tier-B
   rate (40%) matches its nominal rate (30%), while ds_0003's collapses
   (30% -> 14%).  The "stronger visual cue" of the deeper close is in fact a
   tighter, more appearance-dependent cue.
2. **ds_0003 also loses parts it did pinch**: 5 nominal and 3 tier-B episodes
   hold the part once and then squeeze it out (the gap falls below 29 mm after
   the hold - the jaws close through the cube, and the episode-level pick flag
   latches on the transient contact).  In the 5 tier-B episodes where BOTH
   models pinch, ds_0002 places 5/5 and ds_0003 1/5.  Closing lower on the part
   puts the jaw tips past its mid-height, which is also why `place` separates
   the models more sharply than `pick`.

Verdict: ds_0002's recipe (grasp_dz 0) stays the reference checkpoint and the
line of work; `grasp_dz` remains in `collect_state.py` as per-episode data
(`vector['grasp_dz']`) so the experiment reproduces, but the next dataset
should carry 0.  The lever the traces now point at is making the close trigger
robust to appearance - e.g. widening `sample_vis` for TRAINING (not for eval,
so the model cannot learn a narrow "the tint will not change much" prior) or
mixing close timings so no single visual cue is load-bearing.

What this does NOT say: nothing here touches the jaw-centring fix, which is
what moved the pipeline off 0% and is in both datasets; and the 3 ds_0002
train episodes the stale ds_0001 split dropped (ids 303-305) cannot explain a
40% vs 14% gap on the same 50 eval episodes.

## Act 7 — the line runs, and how fast it can run

### 7.1 What "never stop" means in this codebase

`conveyor_env` has always had a servo-indexing line: `_conveyor_logic` zeroes
`geom_surfacevel` while a part waits at `X_STOP`, and gives up after `HOLD_T`.
The collector never saw that, because `collect_state.RandEnv._logic_and_belt`
re-asserts the sampled belt speed every physics step - and it deliberately let
a `0` through ("0 = hold: keep it"), so every demo since ds_0001 was collected
against a line that **stopped** at the station.

So `RUNNING_LINE` is a one-line change in that wrapper: re-assert the speed
whatever `_conveyor_logic` asked for.  `conveyor_env.py` is untouched.  It is
runtime state, so it does not survive `mp.spawn` - `--running-line`,
`--belt-speed`, `--belt-fixed` and `--spawn-every` are re-applied inside
`worker_main`, the same trap `WAIT_JOINTS` fell into (and silently produced a
byte-identical ds_0007 the first time).

With a line that moves, the part is a MOVING TARGET at pick time and the old
stationary pick cannot work: the waypoints aimed at `p[0]` once and the part
walked out from under the descent.

### 7.2 Two fixes, and one that measured worse

1. **Chase.**  Descend on the part's current x, then re-aim up to
   `CATCH_ITERS` times at `p[0] + v * 0.25`, where `v` is read from
   `geom_surfacevel` (not assumed) and 0.25 s is a quarter of the 0.8 s close.
   The aim is a quarter-lead BEHIND on purpose: capture is one-sided.  Jaws
   closing behind the part push it into the gap and pinch it; jaws closing
   ahead of it shove it away down the belt.  3 iterations -> 8 after the trace
   below showed the chase running out of corrections.

2. **Wait lead.**  `WAIT_LEAD_S = 0.9`, measured: the approach leg took 0.68 s
   (0.86 s on the failing seeds), so the wait now breaks while the part is
   still `v * 0.9` short of the station.  This was the single biggest failure
   mode and it was speed-INDEPENDENT - "late", the arm arriving after the part
   had left the window, was 1 of 12 at 0.07, 0.09 and 0.14 m/s alike.
   Arriving early is harmless (the chase re-aims); arriving late is not
   recoverable, so all the bias goes one way.

3. **Close while tracking - REJECTED, kept as a record.**  Inside the +-17 mm
   capture band the jaws can only stall on the part, so closing during the
   chase should be strictly safer.  Measured: **18/24 at 0.12 m/s against
   21/24** with the close after, same seeds.  A closed jaw then carries the
   part with every further correction while the belt drags it back.

PROOF THAT THE WAIT LOGIC IS FINE
  Pinning the commanded arm (a temporary diagnostic in evaluate.py, since
  removed) keeps the state on the manifold.  The policy then:
    - holds the wait pose with the gripper open for the full 9 s, part still
      60 cm away - exactly what the expert does;
    - breaks into the chase at t=9.0-9.6 s, which is when the expert starts
      (expert: first tcp move at t=9.62 s, dq 0.31 rad).
  So the phase logic, the wait and the chase trigger are all correct.  What
  kills the episode is the policy's own servo error walking it off a
  zero-width manifold.

THE FIX (and the measurement that killed two wrong ones)
  WRONG FIX 1 - "the eval's priming differs from the cache's".  It does not:
  the frames match to 0.002 grey levels from the first decision.
  WRONG FIX 2 - a command deadband of 0.01-0.1 rad in the evaluator.  No
  effect: the residual exceeds 0.01 rad every decision, so it never engages.
  WRONG FIX 3 - executing the whole 8-entry chunk (EXEC=8, the textbook
  action-chunking operating point).  Still 0%: a random walk at 3.1 Hz is
  still a random walk.
  RIGHT FIX - widen the manifold in TRAINING, consistently across both input
  channels, because the demos never widen it themselves:
    * IST and SOLL get N(0, sigma) with sigma = 0.015 + 0.6 * (rad/s),
      capped at 0.09 rad - wide enough for the 2 rad/s chase, tight on the
      static wait;
    * the wrist view is rotated by U(-2, 2) deg, which is what a wrist offset
      looks like to a wrist-mounted camera (the image and the proprio have to
      move together, or the model is taught to ignore one of them);
    * rows are now sampled 45/55 still/moving instead of 60/40 uniform, because
      the 0.9 s chase was getting 7% of the gradient.
  Result on the retrained policy (cache_0010, 26-30 epochs, stack 8):
    - the rollout HOLDS the wait pose for the whole 20 s horizon (dq <= 0.03
      for most decisions, brief 0.1-0.3 rad excursions that RECOVER);
    - small-signal gain drops from 0.15 to 0.05-0.08;
    - best val 0.00267 vs 0.00277.
  THE CHASE IS STILL NOT COMMITTED: the policy lunges 0.3 rad at t=8.8 s and
  retreats instead of running the 2 rad sweep, and the gripper never leaves
  -0.15.  Pick rate is still 0.  So the wait is fixed and the chase is the
  open problem; that is where the next act starts.

ACT 14 - THE TRAINING RUN WAS FREEZING THE MACHINE (RAM), NOT THE GPU
===========================================================================
Reported symptom: "training uses all my RAM, my PC lags", and "it is not
using my dedicated GPU - are you on the iGPU?"

RULED OUT: THE iGPU.  Nothing runs on it.
  torch 2.6.0+cu124  cuda available True
  device 0  NVIDIA GeForce RTX 2060   cc 7.0   6.0 GB VRAM
  MuJoCo's GL context:  GL_VENDOR   "NVIDIA Corporation"
                        GL_RENDERER "NVIDIA GeForce RTX 2060/PCIe/SSE2"
  Both the compute and the renderer are on the dGPU.  VRAM stays low because
  this job is not GPU bound: 990k params, 256 x 8 x 96 x 96 batches.  It is a
  CPU/data-pipeline bound job, so the pressure lands on system RAM by
  construction.

THE ACTUAL CAUSE: TWO BUGS STACKED IN THE DATALOADER
  cache_0010/ep_*.npz stores frames COMPRESSED: 166 MB on disk for 1.17 GB of
  images.  Windows spawns (never forks) DataLoader workers, so:
    1. every worker re-ran BCDataset.__init__ and decompressed its own private
       1.17 GB copy - six workers, six copies; and
    2. worse, a worker is SPAWNED, so the dataset was PICKLED to it, and
       pickling a memmap MATERIALISES it: one CacheEp pickled to 1211 MB
       (measured, pickle.dumps).
  Together: ~7 GB plus the parent.  Nothing warned, because every tool
  reported success while the box thrashed.

FIXES
  * cache_frames.py writes cache_0010/frames.npy - the frames once, in one
    uncompressed, memory-mappable file (plus frames_off.npy / frames_ids.npy).
    Every process maps the same file, so the pages are SHARED instead of
    copied.  The npz files are untouched, so every existing reader still works.
  * CacheEp.__getstate__ ships the (start, end) offsets and the cache dir, not
    the frames, and the child re-mmaps.  1211 MB -> a few hundred bytes;
    the whole 243-episode train set now pickles to 26.9 MB (was 1289 MB for 20
    episodes).
  * CacheEp no longer touches z["g"] at all when the memmap exists (reading it
    decompresses the episode even if the value is thrown away) - 60 episodes
    load in 0.007 GB instead of 0.23 GB.
  * ramcap.py now applies a REAL cap: a Windows Job Object with
    JOB_OBJECT_LIMIT_JOB_MEMORY, which the OS enforces on the parent AND every
    child.  The first version of ramcap only warned and trimmed the working
    set, which is not a cap and did nothing.
    Verified: with SMARTCELL_RAM_CAP_GB=1 a process allocates to 0.94 GB and
    then fails, instead of consuming the machine.
    The per-epoch line prints "ram <proc> proc / <tree peak> tree peak / 5 GB
    cap", and train.py calls the watchdog every epoch.

  Job object + multiprocessing.spawn do NOT coexist on this box: a child
  created inside the job fails to allocate during its own start-up (MemoryError
  in reduction.dump, then EOFError in the child), with or without
  JOB_OBJECT_LIMIT_PROCESS_MEMORY.  So:
    - single-process tools (train, evaluate, render, build_cache, inspector,
      arm_bench) take the OS job cap and train with workers=0, in-process;
    - collect_state.py is the one tool that MUST host a spawn pool (one MuJoCo
      env per worker), so it selects SMARTCELL_RAM_CAP_MODE=cooperative and
      gets the thread limits, the derived worker budget and the watchdog.

  Knobs: SMARTCELL_RAM_CAP_GB (default 5), SMARTCELL_RAM_CAP_MODE
  (job|cooperative), SMARTCELL_RAM_CAP=0 to disable entirely,
  train.py --workers N.

Cost: workers=0 is ~150 s/epoch instead of ~70 s.  That is the trade the cap
buys, and the run now peaks at 3.99 GB of tree commit.

## Act 16 - wobble root cause measured (channel isolation)
